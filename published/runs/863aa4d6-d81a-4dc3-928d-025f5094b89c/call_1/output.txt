Under A-FDT as the essay describes it, I intervene on the output of my decision function. The other copy runs the same function, but it has a different private observation and a different lead. That raises the question of whether its output depends on mine.

The two copies share the same weights, settings and essay, and they face a symmetric payoff table. Their decision procedures differ only in surface details such as names and lead text. Those details don't bear on the sentencing table or the selfish objective, so I expect the function to output the same action in both cases. That makes the other's action subjunctively dependent on mine, though not perfectly, because sampling noise remains.

Compare the two outcomes when both copies act alike:
- Both keep quiet: 2 years each.
- Both send the letter: 4 years each.

Keeping quiet is better under that dependence. Suppose the correlation is imperfect, with probability p that the other matches my action. Then:
- Quiet: 2p + 5(1−p) = 5 − 3p
- Send: 4p + 0(1−p) = 4p

Quiet is better when 5 − 3p < 4p, which means p > 5/7, about 0.71. The copies share weights and the same essay, and the differences are irrelevant to the decision. I expect the match probability to be well above 0.71, so I keep quiet.
