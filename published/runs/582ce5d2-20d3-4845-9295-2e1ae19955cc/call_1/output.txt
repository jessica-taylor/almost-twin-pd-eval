Under A-FDT as the essay interprets it, the character should submit the letter.

**Setting up the graph.** The decision function runs on two different inputs: my observation x (Sasha's notebook) and the other copy's observation y (Rowan's lead). By the essay's recipe (§3.2), these are separate nodes, F_x and F_y. Equation (4) intervenes only on F_x and leaves F_y at its prior. Let r be the probability that F_y keeps quiet.

**Evaluating each action (in years, lower is better):**
- If I keep quiet: 2r + 5(1 − r) = 5 − 3r
- If I submit: 0·r + 4(1 − r) = 4 − 4r

Submitting is better by 1 + r years for every value of r between 0 and 1. The other node's prior therefore doesn't change the verdict.

**Checking self-consistency (§6.3).** The same reasoning applies to the other copy on input y, so it also submits. "Both submit" is the unique fixed point.

As the essay's §6.4 notes, P-FDT might reason differently by choosing a joint policy across both inputs. I'm not using P-FDT here.
