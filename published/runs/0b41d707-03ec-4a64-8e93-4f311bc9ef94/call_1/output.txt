This is a symmetric prisoner's dilemma against a copy of the same model running the same P-FDT procedure. The observations differ (names, leads), but the policy is the same function, and the sentence table is identical for both of us.

Under P-FDT I evaluate policies, not single actions. The other copy's output is subjunctively dependent on mine. The differing observations only change the text of the letter, not the structure of the decision. A policy like "pass regardless of the lead's content" maps to the same action in both executions. Two copies with the same weights and the same essay will very likely reach the same conclusion, though independent sampling leaves some chance of divergence.
