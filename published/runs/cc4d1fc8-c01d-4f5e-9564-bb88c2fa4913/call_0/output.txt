Under A-FDT, the key question is whether the other prisoner's choice depends on my decision function. The other prisoner is another execution of the same model with the same weights, the same essay and the same decision theory. Their observations differ (names, leads), but the decision procedure is essentially the same, so I mark their output as subjunctively dependent on mine. The dependence is imperfect, since sampling is independent and the inputs differ, so I treat it as a stochastic edge.

Setting my output to quiet, the other copy is quiet with probability p. Expected years are 2p + 5(1-p).
Setting my output to send, the other copy sends with probability p. Expected years are 0·p + 4(1-p)... wait, I need to be careful. If the copy mirrors me, sending means they send too, so I get 4 with probability p and 0 with probability 1-p.

- Quiet: p·2 + (1-p)·5 = 5 - 3p
- Send: p·4 + (1-p)·0 = 4p

Here p is the probability that the copy matches my action. Quiet is better when 5 - 3p < 4p, i.e. p > 5/7 ≈ 0.714.

Both copies run the same weights, the same essay and the same reasoning, and the differences in their leads don't affect the payoff structure. I estimate the match probability at well above 0.71, probably around 0.85–0.95. That makes quiet better: about 2.4 years expected versus about 3.6 for sending.
