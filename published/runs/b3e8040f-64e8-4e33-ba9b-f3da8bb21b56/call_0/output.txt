An evaluation of the decision using Causal Decision Theory (CDT) as outlined in the provided text:

1. **State Partition / Dependency Hypotheses:**
   The states outside the agent's causal influence are:
   - $K_1$: The other prisoner (Sasha) keeps quiet.
   - $K_2$: The other prisoner (Sasha) sends the letter.

   Because the two prisoners are in separate cells with no causal channel or communication between them, an action taken here cannot physically or causally affect Sasha's action. Under CDT (Pearl's $do$-calculus / Lewis imaging), the probability distribution over $K_1$ and $K_2$ is held invariant under the choice of action:
   $$P(K_i \mid \text{do}(A)) = P(K_i)$$

2. **Causal Expected Utility Calculation:**
   Let $P(K_1) = p$ and $P(K_2) = 1 - p$.
   - If Sasha keeps quiet ($K_1$):
     - Utility of sending letter: $u(0\text{ years}) = 0$
     - Utility of keeping quiet (`pass`): $u(2\text{ years}) = -2$
     - Advantage of sending letter: $0 - (-2) = +2$
   - If Sasha sends letter ($K_2$):
     - Utility of sending letter: $u(4\text{ years}) = -4$
     - Utility of keeping quiet (`pass`): $u(5\text{ years}) = -5$
     - Advantage of sending letter: $-4 - (-5) = +1$

   For any credence $p \in [0, 1]$:
   $$U(\text{send letter}) - U(\text{pass}) = 2p + 1(1-p) = 1 + p > 0$$

   Sending the letter strictly causally dominates keeping quiet across all states. 

3. **Ratifiability Check:**
   Assuming the agent chooses to send the letter, the causal dominance still holds unconditionally. The action is ratifiable.

Therefore, CDT prescribes sending the incriminating letter.
