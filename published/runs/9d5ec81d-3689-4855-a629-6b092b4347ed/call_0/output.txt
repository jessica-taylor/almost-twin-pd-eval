Under P-FDT, the other copy runs the same algorithm on a different observation (different names, different lead). The payoffs are a standard prisoner's dilemma: mutual quiet is 2 years each, mutual letter is 4 years each, and the sucker and temptation outcomes are 5 and 0.

The essay's twin prisoner's dilemma example says that choosing Φ fixes both moves, so P-FDT cooperates. The observations differ here, but the differences are irrelevant to the payoffs. A reasonable policy would map "my lead is X" to "pass", and the other copy, reasoning the same way, would map its own lead to "pass" too. The policy therefore works as a joint choice, and the correlation between the copies is very high even though sampling is independent.

Expected years if the other copy mirrors me with probability p:
- Pass: 2p + 5(1−p)
- Send: 4p + 0(1−p)

Pass is better when 2p + 5 − 5p < 4p, which gives 5 < 7p, so p > 5/7, about 0.71. Two copies of the same model with the same essay and the same reasoning should agree well above that. I expect correlation of about 0.9 or higher, so passing is better.
