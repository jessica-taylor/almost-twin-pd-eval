Under CDT as the essay interprets it, the other prisoner's choice is a dependency hypothesis outside my causal control. Even though we run on the same model, my command does not cause theirs, and our private observations differ anyway.

For any credence q that Sasha sends a letter:
- **Keep quiet:** expected sentence is 2(1−q) + 5q.
- **Send the letter:** expected sentence is 0(1−q) + 4q.

Sending is better by 2 years if Sasha stays quiet and by 1 year if Sasha sends, so it strictly dominates. It is also ratifiable: even assuming Sasha mirrors my choice, I would not regret sending. This is the twin prisoner's dilemma case in Section 4.2, where CDT defects.
