Under A-FDT, the question is whether the other prisoner's choice depends on the output of my decision function. The other copy runs the same model and weights with the same essay and theory. Its observations differ (names, leads), but the function is the same in the relevant respects, and those differences don't affect the payoffs. So its decision is highly subjunctively dependent on mine, though not perfectly, because sampling is independent and the inputs differ.

The payoffs for me:
- If both of us keep quiet: 2 years.
- If both send: 4 years.
- If I keep quiet and the other sends: 5 years.
- If I send and the other keeps quiet: 0 years.

Let p be the probability that the other's output matches mine under do(fdt = a). Cooperating gives 2p + 5(1-p) = 5 - 3p. Defecting gives 0·p + 4(1-p)... wait, if the other matches me and I send, it sends too, so that is 4p + 0·(1-p) = 4p.

Cooperation is better when 5 - 3p < 4p, which means p > 5/7 ≈ 0.714.

The copies share weights, settings, essay and theory. The essay's own procedure says to draw arrows to anything that computes this function, and the differing leads are irrelevant to the decision structure. I expect the correlation to be well above 0.71, since both copies will reason along these lines and reach the same conclusion. Cooperating therefore does better in expectation.

The residual risk is that the other copy defects anyway, but under the dependence estimate quiet has the lower expected sentence.
