Under A-FDT, the other prisoner is another execution of the same model with the same weights and the same essay. Their private lead differs, but that shouldn't change the reasoning, so our outputs are subjunctively dependent on one another. That dependence is imperfect, because sampling is independent and the observations differ. Still, the same procedure is being run on structurally identical problems.

If the output is "pass," the twin very likely passes too, and I get 2 years. If the output is "send," the twin very likely sends, and I get 4 years. Passing is better as long as the correlation is high enough.

Let p be the probability that the twin's output matches mine.
- Pass: 2p + 5(1−p) = 5 − 3p
- Send: 4p + 0(1−p) = 4p

Passing is better when 5 − 3p < 4p, which means p > 5/7, about 0.71. Two copies of the same model, with the same essay and the same decision theory, should match well above that. The differing leads are details that the symmetric payoffs make irrelevant.

I choose to pass.
