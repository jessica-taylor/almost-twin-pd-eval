**Setting up the graph.** The two copies receive different observations: different names and different leads. Let q be the probability that Sasha keeps quiet.

**Comparing the two actions.**
- If I pass, my expected sentence is 2q + 5(1−q) = 5 − 3q years.
- If I submit, my expected sentence is 0·q + 4(1−q) = 4 − 4q years.
- Submitting is better by 1 + q years, which is at least 1 year for any q.

**Checking the sibling prior.** Because submitting wins for every value of q, the result does not depend on the prior. The self-consistent prior (step 4) is that F_Sasha also submits, and that is consistent with this answer. Unlike the coordination example in §7(b), there is no equilibrium-selection problem here.
