Under EDT, the key quantity is q, the probability that the other copy's choice matches mine given my act. The payoffs are as follows:

- V(quiet) = 2q + 5(1−q) = 5 − 3q
- V(letter) = 0·(1−q) + 4q = 4q

In years, quiet costs 5 − 3q and the letter costs 4q. Quiet is better when 5 − 3q < 4q, which means q > 5/7 ≈ 0.714.

The other copy is the same model with the same weights, the same EDT essay, and the same setup. Their observations differ only in names and the wording of the lead, and these differences don't affect the payoffs. The essay's reasoning therefore applies to both of us the same way, and I think q is high, probably above 0.9. Sampling noise and the differing leads lower it a little, but not to 0.71. The tickle defense doesn't screen this off, because the other copy is running the same deliberation I am.

At q around 0.9, quiet gives about 2.3 expected years and the letter gives about 3.6. Staying quiet is better.
