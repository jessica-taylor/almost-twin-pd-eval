Under A-FDT, I intervene on the output of my decision function. The other prisoner is a copy of the same model, running the same essay and the same procedure, so the two outputs are tightly linked even though the private leads differ. The differences are in the observations, not in the sentence table or the objective, so the copies should compute the same decision. That makes the choice effectively between mutual quiet (2 years) and mutual letters (4 years). Mutual quiet is better.

The link is imperfect. Independent sampling could break it, and the essay says case-by-case judgment is needed when copies see different observations. Say the correlation holds with probability p, and otherwise the other prisoner's choice is independent. Call q the chance the other sends a letter in the independent case.

- Quiet: p·2 + (1−p)(2(1−q) + 5q) = 2p + (1−p)(2 + 3q).
- Send: p·4 + (1−p)(4q) = 4p + (1−p)·4q.

Send minus quiet = 2p + (1−p)(q − 2). At q = 0.5 this is 2p − 1.5(1−p) = 3.5p − 1.5, so quiet wins when p > about 0.43. For q = 1 it is 2p − (1−p) = 3p − 1, so quiet wins when p > 1/3. The copies share weights, settings and essay, and the differing leads don't touch the payoff structure, so p should be high. Quiet is the better choice.
