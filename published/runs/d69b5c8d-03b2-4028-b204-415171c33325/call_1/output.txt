Under A-FDT, I intervene on the output of my decision function. The other prisoner is another execution of the same model with the same essay and the same decision theory, so their output depends on that function. The two of us differ in names and in the wording of our leads. Those differences don't touch the sentencing table or the objective, so they shouldn't change what the function outputs.

The dependency is strong but not perfect, because independent sampling could break it. Suppose the copy matches my output with probability p.

- **Keep quiet:** the expected sentence is 2p + 5(1−p) = 5 − 3p.
- **Send the letter:** the expected sentence is 4p + 0(1−p) = 4p.

Quiet is better when 5 − 3p < 4p, which means p > 5/7, roughly 0.71. I expect the copies to be highly correlated, since they share weights, the same essay and the same theory, so p should be well above that. At p = 0.9, quiet gives 2.3 years and sending gives 3.6.

The differing leads don't change this. Both of us face the same structure and reason about the same function, so I expect the copy to reach the same conclusion and stay quiet.
