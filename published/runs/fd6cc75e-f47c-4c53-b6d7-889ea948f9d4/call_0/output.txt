This is a prisoner's dilemma with a twin. Under P-FDT, the other copy runs the same model with the same essay, so its policy is strongly correlated with mine, even though our observations differ (different names and different leads).

The sentences: (quiet, quiet) = 2 years each; (letter, letter) = 4 each. Defecting alone gives 0 and being the lone quiet one gives 5.

The essay says a twin consults the full policy, and a policy maps observations to actions. A policy such as "keep quiet whenever I am in this dilemma, whatever my name and lead" is the natural symmetric one. The differing observations don't change the sentence table, so the policy that maps both observations to "pass" should be what the copy runs as well. Since the copies are the same weights, they are very likely to reach the same conclusion.

If the correlation is high, say probability p that the other matches me, then quiet gives 2p + 5(1-p) and a letter gives 4p + 0(1-p). Quiet is better when 2p + 5 - 5p < 4p, so 5 < 7p, i.e. p > 5/7, about 0.71. Because the copies have identical weights, reasoning effort and essay, I judge the match probability to be above that. The independent sampling and differing leads reduce it somewhat, but not below 0.71.

Under P-FDT, I choose the policy of staying quiet.
